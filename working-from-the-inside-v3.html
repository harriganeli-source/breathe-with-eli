Working from
the Inside

A training series for spaceholders

Starting September 2026 • Victor, NY • 8 spots • $950

Keep Me Posted

Most people who hold space for others are well-trained in how to work with the people in front of them.

Fewer have spent real time examining what happens in their own nervous system during a session—and how much that shapes the outcome.

When someone isn't progressing, there's a pull to do something.
When the silence goes too long, there's an urge to fill it.
When you sense disapproval or resistance, the need to earn your way back
can be almost compulsive.

Therapists know this territory as countertransference.

What most training doesn't cover is what it actually feels like in the body—and what to do with it while it's happening.

Your ability to be present
with whatever is actually happening in you
is the most significant thing
you bring into the room.

Decades of research on what actually helps people change
points to the same conclusion:

It's not the modality or the technique.
It's the quality of the relationship.

And the quality of the relationship starts with you.

That includes the discomfort, the doubt, the urge to do something.
Most of us were never taught to work with those things directly,
and most of us can feel the gap.

This series is designed to close that gap.

Over four sessions we'll use breathwork, partner exercises, relational practices, and structured self-inquiry to help you locate where you leave yourself in session—where you're acting from need rather than presence, and where you're working harder than the work requires.

You'll leave with a clearer felt sense of what's yours and what belongs to the person you're with, and more trust in your ability to be effective through presence alone.

The work will change how you show up in session.

It tends to change how you show up everywhere else too.

What the series covers
  • 01The physiology of the helping impulse
  • 02Presence as a clinical tool
  • 03Reading the room through the body
  • 04Countertransference from the inside out
  • 05Intuition, and when to trust it
  • 06Knowing when to act and when to wait

Who This Is For

Whether you're a therapist, a coach, a social worker, a nurse, a teacher, a bodyworker, or a member of the clergy—

if your work puts you in regular contact with other people's pain, this series is for you.

You don't need a credential for this to matter.
The skills translate to any relationship, including the ones waiting for you at home.

What we'll go into
  • The pull to fix, and where it lives in the body
  • The anxiety that surfaces when a session isn't moving
  • What it feels like to stay present with someone else's pain without trying to change it
  • The wounds that draw people to helping work, and how they show up in the room
  • The difference between helping from wholeness and helping from need
  • What becomes available when we stop trying to manage our reactions and start reading them instead
What the Sessions Look Like

We meet four Sunday mornings in the fall, each session running three hours.

The work includes breathwork, partner exercises, relational practices, journaling, and group discussion. Sessions open creating space to notice in real time where there's a reach, a withdrawal, a moment of working harder than the moment requires. Discussion and journaling run throughout, giving time to integrate what surfaces.

Your internal state is information.

The tightening, the pull toward a client or away from them, the flatness that descends out of nowhere. The breathwork surfaces what's actually running underneath—the anxiety, the need, the patterns we've learned to work around rather than through.

Your body is tracking something in the room.
Learning to read it changes what's available to you in session.

This is experiential work. It will ask something of you.
About Eli

Eli Harrigan is a breathwork facilitator and somatic coach based in Rochester, NY.

He trained with Jesse Torgerson, who has been facilitating this work around the world for over 40 years, and has been working with individuals, groups, and organizations since 2019.

He also facilitates the Present Man Project, a men's group focused on embodied self-inquiry and relational development.
