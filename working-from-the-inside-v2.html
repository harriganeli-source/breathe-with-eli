Working from the Inside

For therapists and other spaceholders

Fall 2026 • Victor, NY • 8 spots • $950

Keep Me Posted

Most people in the helping professions are well-trained in how to work with the people in front of them. Fewer have spent real time examining what happens in their own nervous system during a session, and how much that shapes the outcome.

When a client isn't progressing, there's a pull to do something. When the silence goes too long, there's an urge to fill it. When you sense disapproval or resistance, the need to earn your way back can be almost compulsive. This is the part of the work that often doesn't show up in supervision, isn't always addressed in continuing education, and quietly runs your sessions more than you probably realize.

Your ability to be present with whatever is actually happening in you is the most clinically significant thing you bring into the room.

More than your training, your modality, or your technique. That includes the discomfort, the doubt, the urge to do something. Most of us were never taught to work with those things directly, and most of us can feel the gap.

This series is designed to close it.

What the series covers
  • The physiology of the helping impulse
  • Presence as a clinical tool
  • Reading the room through the body
  • Countertransference from the inside out
  • Intuition, and when to trust it
  • Knowing when to act and when to wait

Over four sessions we'll use breathwork, partner exercises, relational practices, and structured self-inquiry to help you locate where you leave yourself in session, where you're acting from need rather than presence, and where you're working harder than the work requires.

You'll leave with a clearer felt sense of what's yours and what belongs to the person you're with, and more trust in your ability to be effective through presence alone.

Who This Is For

Primarily designed for:

  • Therapists and psychotherapists
  • Social workers
  • Coaches
  • Healthcare workers
  • Clergy
  • Teachers

And anyone else whose work puts them in regular contact with other people's pain. Yoga teachers, bodyworkers, first responders. You don't need a credential for this to matter. The skills translate to any relationship, including the ones waiting for you at home.

What we'll go into
  • The pull to fix, and where it lives in the body
  • The anxiety that surfaces when a session isn't moving
  • What it feels like to stay present with someone else's pain without trying to change it
  • The wounds that draw people to helping work, and how they show up in the room
  • The difference between helping from wholeness and helping from need
  • What becomes available when we stop trying to manage our reactions and start reading them instead
What the Sessions Look Like

We meet four Sunday mornings in the fall, each session running three hours. The work includes breathwork, partner exercises, relational practices, journaling, and group discussion. Sessions open with partner exercises and relational practices, creating space to notice in real time where there's a reach, a withdrawal, a moment of working harder than the moment requires. Discussion and journaling run throughout, giving time to integrate what surfaces.

The breathwork surfaces what's actually running underneath the helping impulse — the anxiety, the need, the patterns we've learned to work around rather than through. Your internal state is information. The tightening, the pull toward a client or away from them, the flatness that descends out of nowhere. Your body is tracking something in the room, and learning to read it changes what's available to you in session.

This is experiential work. It will ask something of you.

About Eli
