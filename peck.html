The Long Work

A guided inquiry into M. Scott Peck’s The Road Less Traveled

Reserve Your Spot

“The attempt to avoid legitimate suffering lies at the root of all emotional illness.”

— M. Scott Peck

Most people have heard of this book. Fewer have actually finished it.

Peck opens with three words: Life is difficult. Not as a warning—as a relief. Because once you stop fighting that fact, something loosens. The suffering doesn’t go away. But your relationship to it changes.

I’ve been sitting with this book for a long time. There’s something in it that keeps asking to be returned to. Not studied—felt. And I think it works better in a room with other people than it does alone on your couch.

So I’m running a small group on Zoom starting May 6th. Six sessions, every other Wednesday evening. We’ll move through the book slowly, one section at a time. I’m a somatic coach and group facilitator, and I’ll be bringing that into every session—which means we won’t just be talking about Peck’s ideas. We’ll be finding where they actually live. In your body. In your relationships. In the patterns you’ve been meaning to look at for a while now.

What We’ll Work Through

Module 1 — Discipline & Love Sessions 1–3 · May 6, May 20, June 3

Responsibility. Delaying gratification. Dedication to truth. And Peck’s dismantling of almost everything most of us were taught about what love actually is.

Module 2 — Growth, Religion & Grace Sessions 4–6 · June 17, July 1, July 15

The maps we use to navigate reality, and what it costs to hold onto ones that are wrong. And grace: the thing that tends to show up when you stop white-knuckling it.

Every session is recorded and sent to you afterward, so you have it regardless of whether you can make it live.

About Eli

I’m a somatic coach and group facilitator based in Rochester, NY. I’ve been doing this work long enough to know that most of us understand a lot more than we’ve actually integrated. There’s a gap between the insight and the shift—and that gap is where I work.

I’m not running this group because I’ve got Peck figured out. I’m running it because I think it’s the kind of book that opens differently when you’re sitting with other people who are trying to be honest.
