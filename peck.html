The Work of Growing Up

A guided inquiry into M. Scott Peck’s The Road Less Traveled, using the book as a doorway into personal work.

“The attempt to avoid legitimate suffering lies at the root of all emotional illness.”

— M. Scott Peck
Register

The first sentence of The Road Less Traveled is “Life is difficult.” The book is about coming to terms with that, rather than trying to change it or avoid it.

Peck says things in this book that nobody says anymore. It is quietly provocative in a way that feels needed right now.

A lot of people have this book on their shelf, or have had it recommended to them and never made the time. This group is for them.

What We’ll Work Through

Seven meetings, moving through all four sections of the book.

Part I: Discipline

Peck’s four tools for facing life without flinching: delaying gratification, accepting responsibility, dedication to truth, and balancing.

Part II: Love

Peck redefines love as a decision and an act of will, an extension of yourself toward another person’s growth.

Part III: Growth and Religion

The maps we use to navigate reality: how we built them, and what it costs to hold onto ones that are wrong.

Part IV: Grace

The strangest and, for many people, the most valuable section. Peck tries to name the force that shows up when you stop white-knuckling it.

I find myself referencing it again and again, in sessions, in conversations, and quietly to myself. Running this group is a way of finally giving that obsession a proper home.

This is not a traditional book club. We’ll use the ideas as a doorway into personal inquiry, applying them to what’s actually happening in our lives: our relationships, our patterns, our struggles. Every session has space for questions and real conversation.
