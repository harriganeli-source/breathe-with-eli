The Work of Growing Up

A guided inquiry into M. Scott Peck’s The Road Less Traveled, using the book as a doorway into personal work.

Reserve Your Spot

“The attempt to avoid legitimate suffering lies at the root of all emotional illness.”

— M. Scott Peck

Most people have heard of this book. Fewer have actually finished it.

Peck opens with three words: Life is difficult. Not as a warning, but as a relief. Because once you stop fighting that fact, something loosens. The suffering doesn’t go away. But your relationship to it changes.

Each session moves between the text and personal inquiry. We’ll spend time with the material and then work with how it applies to what’s actually happening in your life: your relationships, your patterns, the things you keep bumping into. Every session has space for questions. The book is a doorway, and that’s how we’ll use it.

I’ve been sitting with this book for a long time. It comes up in my work constantly: in sessions, in conversations, in the middle of talking about something else entirely, because the ideas in it are that close to the bone. What it opened for me, and keeps opening, is an honest relationship with difficulty. Running this group is partly a way of finally giving that obsession a proper home. And I think it works better in a room with other people than it does alone on your couch.

So I’m running a small group on Zoom starting May 5th. Eight sessions, every other Tuesday evening. We’ll move through the book slowly, one section at a time. I’m a somatic coach and group facilitator, and I’ll be bringing that into every session, which means we won’t just be talking about Peck’s ideas. We’ll be finding where they actually live. In your body. In your relationships. In the patterns you’ve been meaning to look at for a while now.

Your spot is yours for the full eight weeks. The sessions are recorded and sent to you after each one, so if life gets in the way of a Tuesday night, nothing is lost.

What We’ll Work Through

Eight meetings, moving through all four sections of the book.

Part I: Discipline

Peck’s four tools for facing life without flinching: delaying gratification, accepting responsibility, dedication to truth, and balancing. The responsibility chapter alone has a way of sitting with people for weeks.

Part II: Love

Peck redefines love as a decision and an act of will, an extension of yourself toward another person’s growth. Most of us were taught something easier than this, and most of us have felt the gap between that version and what love actually requires.

Part III: Growth and Religion

The maps we use to navigate reality: how we built them, and what it costs to hold onto ones that are wrong. Peck isn’t talking about organized religion here. He’s talking about the worldview each of us carries without realizing it.

Part IV: Grace

The strangest and, for many people, the most valuable section. Peck tries to name the force that shows up when you stop white-knuckling it. Some people find it the most important part of the book. Some aren’t ready for it the first time through. Either way, we’ll go there.

About Eli

I’m a somatic coach and group facilitator based in Rochester, NY. I’ve been doing this work long enough to know that most of us understand a lot more than we’ve actually integrated. There’s a gap between the insight and the shift, and that gap is where I work.

I’m not running this group because I’ve got Peck figured out. I’m running it because I think it’s the kind of book that opens differently when you’re sitting with other people who are trying to be honest.

The section on responsibility is the one that got me. I read it at a point in my life when I was very good at explaining why things weren’t my fault, and it took the ground out from under that. I’ve been chewing on it ever since.
